Where the gaps—and alternatives—are clearest

Housing · Mixed

Supply is rising; affordability remains in crisis

Completions increased, but homelessness, prices and long delivery times remain serious weaknesses. Alternatives include a larger State construction role, modular factories and additional social and affordable housing targets proposed by the Social Democrats, Sinn Féin and People Before Profit; Labour proposes a State construction company and a bulk-purchase stamp duty.

Missed opportunity to test: faster public/cost-rental delivery and tenure-specific affordability targets alongside private supply.
Health · Mixed

Strong population health, weak planned-care access

Healthy life expectancy compares well internationally, but hundreds of thousands remain on planned-care waiting lists. Alternatives include additional beds and medical-card expansion (Sinn Féin), more Sláintecare and staffing investment (Social Democrats), expanded child GP care (Labour) and additional acute capacity (People Before Profit).

Missed opportunity to test: publish workforce-backed waiting-time and treatment-capacity plans with measurable specialty targets.
Climate · Off track

Emissions are falling too slowly for the 2030 target

Emissions fell for a fourth consecutive year, but the reduction to date remains far short of the statutory trajectory. Alternatives include Solar for All and longer-term energy-cost measures (Social Democrats), plug-in solar and energy credits (Labour), and other parties' energy-credit and price proposals.

Missed opportunity to test: a funded sector-by-sector acceleration plan, grid delivery milestones and independently measured retrofit and transport outcomes.
Justice & rural environment · Mixed

Important warning signs need a stronger response

Survey victimisation and fraud have risen while confidence in justice effectiveness has fallen. Farm-income forecasts and river-nitrogen measures also point to pressure in rural areas. The current assessment records alternatives as an evidence gap here: it does not yet have sufficiently verified, comparable party proposals to claim a specific alternative would outperform Government.

Missed opportunity to test: publish detection, court-delay and victim-support measures; pair farm supports with catchment-level water-quality outcomes.

Method: 2 positive, 5 mixed and 1 off-track verdict among 8 areas with an assessable overall direction; 3 areas explicitly say the evidence is insufficient for an overall rating. The 54-row commitment register contains 4 “in progress” and 50 “not verifiable” records. Party alternatives are proposals, not independently proven outcomes; their cost, legality, capacity and delivery risks still require scrutiny.

          Scoring: exact broad-category matches receive 2 points; adjacent categories receive 1; other categories receive 0.           Editorial standards

          How we assess policy

          The aim is not to tell you what to think. Each conclusion is provisional, tied to dated evidence and explicit limitations. The site distinguishes commitments, inputs, implementation outputs and outcomes; it does not assign a single overall government score.

          01

          Separate promise from delivery

          We distinguish announcements, legislation, funding, implementation outputs and real-world outcomes.

          02

          Show competing arguments fairly

          Left- and right-leaning arguments are presented as perspectives to test, not as labels that determine whether a claim is true.

          03

          Attribute alternatives

          Party proposals are attributed to the party publishing them and are not described as enacted Government policy.

          04

          Make uncertainty visible

          Where evidence is incomplete, outdated or not directly comparable, the limitation is stated rather than hidden.

          05

          Use transparent conclusions

          Conclusions distinguish positive, mixed, off-track and not-yet-rateable findings. The judgement is tied to the stated indicators, not to party identity.

          06

          Disclose missing evidence

          Every policy assessment lists the next evidence needed. Missing data are not filled with assumptions or treated as proof of no action.

          07

          Keep party claims attributable

          Party proposals are linked to their publisher where available; secondary-source summaries and unverified costings are labelled.

          08

          Compare systems carefully

          International examples are used to frame policy choices, not as simple rankings. Differences in institutions, law, geography and funding are stated.
